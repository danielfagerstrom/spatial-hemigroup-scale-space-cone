**Part B: presentation review**

*Spatial scale space from hemigroup axioms: the admissible cone, its corners and their implementation*, build `a76a323`, 73 pages. Reviewed 19 September 2026 in Codex; the reasoning setting is not exposed in this session and is therefore recorded as unreported. Page references below are the PDF's printed page numbers, which coincide with its page indices.

The manuscript communicates a substantial programme, and the sentence beginning “For an imaging reader the gain is concrete” (p. 2) does identify a computational reason to care. After three pages, a reader knows the main subjects and the attraction of recursive non-Gaussian smoothing. The reader is less secure about the operational meaning of “exact,” the restrictions on recursive realization, and the separation between a theory on the continuous line and a sampled implementation. The abstract supplies too many results at the same level of emphasis; the body repeatedly alternates between explaining the mathematics and documenting the formalization project. The strongest explanatory passages connect a formula directly to a filtering operation. The revision should make that connection the organizing principle.

I read the complete manuscript and inspected its rendered pages, including all ten figures and the tables. The twenty items below are ranked by their effect on comprehension and readiness for an external readership. They concern presentation; they do not certify the mathematical statements, proofs, or numerical conclusions.

1. **Put the meaning and limits of “exact” beside the first computational promise.** On p. 1, “the cascade itself is the algorithm” and “computed stage by stage over any finite set of scales it is exact” invite an implementation reading. Page 3 does mention the integer-index restriction, but the abstract does not; the continuous-space qualification arrives clearly on p. 54 and the sampled construction on p. 62. Bring those distinctions together in the opening. Suggested wording: “The cascade composes exactly across any chosen finite set of scales for continuous-space signals. For integer-index Matérn kernels, its transitions have first-order forward–backward realizations; spatial sampling is a separate approximation.” Keep the exclusions concerning locality and extrema, and bring the one-dimensional scope forward from p. 4. This is a request to make the scope legible, independently of Part A's assessment of the claims.

2. **Give the abstract and introduction a hierarchy of results.** The abstract's “This paper describes that class” (p. 1) opens an inventory of cone geometry, an origin threshold, subordination, three families, a characterization, generators, implementation, scope, and formalization. On pp. 2–3, the five result paragraphs repeat much of that inventory with proposition numbers. Lead with the computational problem and three contributions: the class's representation, the relation to established kernel families, and what can be computed recursively. Move the origin threshold and detailed closure description out of the abstract. Turn “This paper describes that class” into “We describe how these kernels are represented, how familiar families fit within the class, and which admit recursive scale transitions.” A compact comparison table near §5 would carry more practical information than another catalogue of results.

3. **Consolidate the formalization account and translate its internal vocabulary.** Section 1.1 occupies much of pp. 4–6 before the mathematical preliminaries. Table 1 earns a place as an account of dependencies, but its ledger identifiers and long caption are better in a formalization appendix, with a short scope summary retained up front. “Its lemma, typed and priced” (p. 6) has no intelligible journal meaning; replace it with “Lemma 4.12 is stated in the development but its proof is not machine-checked.” Explain or remove “interface,” “consumer,” “node,” and “proof of record” when they concern the development rather than mathematical objects. Retain qualifications needed to interpret statements. Delete or correct “Nothing outside §1.1 comments on the machine checking” (p. 69): the repeated discussions on, for example, pp. 20, 46, 50 and 68 visibly contradict that editorial description.

4. **Remove internal acquisition notes from the bibliography.** Reference [32] on p. 72 prints “search engines unreachable from sandbox” and instructions to obtain institutional access. References [41] and [42] on pp. 72–73 print filing history and, in [41], OCR search advice. These are working-library records, not bibliographic information. Move them to the repository's source ledger; retain conventional publication details and usable access links where appropriate. Reference [13] on p. 71 likewise needs an ordinary versioned repository citation instead of the label “Acquisition note.” Fix the visibly printed “Statistics &amp; Probability Letters” in [24], p. 71. These are conspicuous publication-preparation defects, not matters of taste.

5. **Redraw Figure 4 so that geometry does not assert unknown memberships.** The caption on p. 30 says “nothing is claimed ... about the place of the Cin rays among those classes,” yet the Cin point is drawn in the region between the boundaries of A₁ and A₂. The distinct nested regions for S and A∞ similarly look like a strict inclusion even though the caption says equality is open. A dashed boundary does not explain those conventions by itself. Use an inclusion diagram with explicit labels such as “equality unresolved,” and locate witnesses only where their memberships are asserted. If a second panel is wanted, plot the polar profiles for the witnesses of pp. 33–35; that would explain the dimensional distinction more concretely than additional ovals.

6. **Reduce the competing vocabularies and identify which measure is being discussed.** “The profile k is a catalogue of jumps” (p. 2, capitalization adjusted), the “displacement dictionary” (p. 9), and “a catalogue whose jump sizes lie on a lattice” (p. 29) make “catalogue” carry too much. The reader needs a stable distinction between the profile k, the jump measure ν(dx) = k(x)dx/x, and the step-superposition measure ϖ = −dk. State their roles together, then name the relevant object instead of calling all of them a catalogue. Keep “profile” and the explicitly defined “stage.” Replace “record” by “smoothed signal,” or define it at p. 1 rather than waiting for “Call the smoothed signal at a scale the record” on p. 54. Use “classical families” for “corners”: readers should not have to discover on p. 16 that the named families generally are not extreme rays. “Subordination map” is a clearer default than “bridge”; “lag section” is adequately glossed at p. 56.

7. **Make Figure 10's sign information visible in the plot itself.** Its p. 64 caption describes Deriche's transfer “in modulus, with its three sign changes.” A modulus plot can show zeros or dips, but cannot show which intervals have negative transfer. Mark the reported zero locations and distinguish negative intervals, or add a small signed panel. The insets showing negative profile and increment values are useful and should stay. Enlarge the legends, ticks and inset labels: they are small relative to the body text, despite the generous unused space on this figure page. Give the panels letters and state their comparison conventions individually; “at σ = 1” and the varying cascade order are easier to follow when attached to the appropriate panels.

8. **Give Figure 9 a quantitative visual key and separate its two stories.** The p. 63 caption says the difference images use “one gray scale that saturates at ±0.055,” but there is no displayed grayscale key identifying negative, zero and positive differences. Add one with labelled endpoints and zero. State the equal-bandwidth comparison in the caption, including what the display scale means; its definition is currently back on p. 62. The scale-space images and the quadrature-convergence plot answer different questions. Move the latter into its own figure near the discussion on pp. 65–66, allowing readable legends and a direct explanation of the dotted search-result line. This figure is important because it shows signals rather than only kernels; it needs clearer decoding, not removal.

9. **Make Table 2 an index for this paper and eliminate the avoidable B collision.** Its caption on p. 7 explicitly assigns B both to the exponent symbol and to Brownian motion. Use, for example, Wᵤ for Brownian motion and keep B(ω) for the symbol. The argument/subscript convention is decipherable, but creates unnecessary work in a paper already switching between processes, transforms and operators. Table 2 promises “where it is defined,” yet several rows lack a precise locator, while important later notation is deliberately omitted. Provide columns for symbol, meaning and definition location; include the Thorin measure U, the class S, the dimension classes and the family parameters. Move the causal-paper translation column to §4, where readers actually need that comparison. Give locally introduced symbols a verbal gloss before using them in introductory summaries, especially “Thorin measure” on p. 3.

10. **Remove the unexplained second-paper machinery from the end of §4.** Remark 4.13, pp. 36–37, introduces “the memory line,” “the causal signaling form and its embodied jet,” and “the inversion operator of the memory half-line.” This is the sharpest break with the promise of a self-contained paper. These objects are neither needed for the preceding construction nor explained well enough to carry the comparison. End §4 with the map's established relationships and limitations. If the connection to later work must remain, one sentence suffices: “The corresponding relation between local equations is deferred to the next paper.” The title “What else crosses the bridge” should not license a page of prerequisites belonging to another project module.

11. **Keep section maps that explain dependencies; shorten maps that repeat statements.** The “Why the bridge” paragraph at p. 21 helps because it explains why §4 is needed. The opening of §6, p. 51, also helps by connecting kernels to evolution equations. By contrast, “The description has four steps, one subsection each” (pp. 11–12), “The results, in the order of the subsections” (p. 37), and the long list on p. 55 duplicate the introduction and the next subsection openings. Reduce each to its question, principal answer, and reading route. Section 2 should open with “Definitions and results used below,” rather than the history of “What the line paper settled” (p. 6); merge the tiny §2.5 into the formalization note. Also correct “The four statements” (p. 54) followed by a five-item map on p. 55, and represent §7.5 if the list is meant as a subsection map.

12. **Replace “machine” with an explicit picture of the two approximation operations.** “The Thorin subclass as a machine” (p. 59) does not name an algorithm a reader can recognize. “Finite Thorin approximations and recursive realization” would. The paragraph's useful distinction, “The convergence is that of the quadratures with real weights. The rounding is a second step,” deserves a small diagram: the target measure leads to finite quadratures with real weights; a separate arrow changes those weights to even integers and produces a recursively realizable family. Label the first convergence and the second change of target, following the text's stated qualifications. This is the most useful missing algorithmic figure. Reuse it to introduce the numerical comparison rather than making readers reconstruct the distinction from §§7.4 and 7.6.

13. **Simplify or remove Figure 2's pseudo-geometric placement of families.** The caption on p. 15 says “nothing is placed by the drawing,” while the drawing places stable, Matérn and Student-t labels on different rays in a shaded region with a visible boundary. A reader naturally tries to interpret those positions, and the disclaimer withdraws the very information the image seems to offer. Keep a small schematic solely for the superposition of the Gaussian and Cin rays, or replace this figure with a clearly labelled class-inclusion diagram coordinated with Figure 4 and the chain on p. 43. One diagram should explain generating rays; the other should explain class membership. The current hybrid does neither cleanly enough to justify its long caption.

14. **Keep Figure 1, but show the feature that its caption asks readers to see.** “A Cin ray, three ways” (p. 14) is a good organizing device: profile, exponent and kernel belong together. The caption says the kernel “shows echoes of the singularity at ±τ and ±2τ,” but at page size the central peak dominates and the marked positions are much easier to see than any change of smoothness. Add a magnified neighbourhood or a suitable derivative view, retaining the stated numerical/illustrative status. Enlarge the three panels' legends and annotations, and add line styles so the profiles and exponent curves remain distinguishable in grayscale. Shorten the caption's result-by-result cross-referencing after the relevant visual features have been labelled directly.

15. **Make Figure 5 explain its normalization and its limits as a comparison.** On p. 38, “The three corner families at canonical scale 1” sounds like a common smoothing comparison, but the caption itself notes that increasing the Student-t parameter also narrows its kernel. The Matérn and stable panels vary their own parameters under different conventions. Put “parameters vary at fixed canonical scale; bandwidths are not matched” directly above the figure or at the start of the caption. If the aim is practical comparison, provide a common bandwidth convention as in §7.6; otherwise keep this explicitly as a family-shape illustration. Linear density plots over this short interval do not make tail orders visible. Put the tail, moment, origin-behaviour and recursive-realization information in the comparison table suggested in item 2. The small legends also need enlargement and grayscale-safe styles.

16. **Retain Figures 3, 6 and 7, which each explain a distinct operation.** “The bridge on two corners” (Figure 3, p. 26) gives useful before-and-after distributions; add a visible mixture arrow between columns so it depicts the operation as well as its inputs and outputs. “The jump part of the generator” (Figure 6, p. 52) earns its compact space by turning the formula into neighbour comparisons; make its line-weight convention readable, since the Matérn and stable cartoons currently look very similar. “The cascade as an algorithm” (Figure 7, p. 55) is the clearest computational schematic and deserves an early introduction cross-reference. Replace its ambiguous “×γ” marks with “repeat γ times,” since multiplication by a gain is also a natural reading of a filter diagram.

17. **Remove sampling cues that Figure 8 immediately disclaims, and move it out of the proposition.** The p. 58 caption says the sample marks “recall the subsampling of multiresolution practice” but that “the statement involves no sampling.” Remove the dots or put a visibly separate, explicitly illustrative subsampling panel elsewhere. The scale ladder and interpolating family are enough for this figure's purpose, and the right-hand profile usefully adds information. The current float also intervenes between the formula in Proposition 7.4 on p. 57 and its qualification “it is admissible exactly when this k is nonincreasing” on p. 58. Keep the whole statement together and place the figure before or after it. Hypotheses and conclusions should not have to be read around an illustration.

18. **Give the numerical threshold table a standalone caption and explicit weight convention.** On p. 65, “two atoms, weights 0.3 and 0.5” is not self-explanatory after the manuscript has repeatedly distinguished Thorin weights from exponent weights. Identify the measure to which these weights belong, put the two ranges in the caption rather than the following paragraph, and label the entries as truncated inverse-transform values. Number the table so it can be cited. It earns its place: the three cutoffs allow a compact comparison of growth across regimes. A visual grouping of the rows by c < 1, c = 1 and c > 1 would make that comparison faster. Retain Table 1 as a dependency reference in an appendix and improve Table 2 as in item 9; the tables need distinct jobs rather than more running explanation.

19. **Replace announcements and self-commentary with the statement they introduce.** “What it has is an equation” (p. 17) can become “Its density satisfies a delay equation.” “Three things are to be shown about Ψ, and the lemma shows them” (p. 23) can be replaced by the three actual properties. “Two steps of that paragraph deserve to be said out loud” (p. 26) can become “Two identifications require justification.” “Neither is settled in the held literature” (p. 32) needs a reader-facing description of the cited literature, not the author's holdings. The sentence ending “this reading is a reading” on that page should say plainly: “The interpretation in terms of higher-dimensional scale-space families is heuristic; only the exponent condition is used here.” These edits remove verbal scaffolding while preserving useful explanation.

20. **End sections on the result's use, and shorten the conclusion and process account.** Section 6 ends cleanly with its last generator computation (p. 54), and §7 ends with a useful distinction between the needs of single-scale and multiscale users (p. 67). Section 5 instead ends by explaining which proof route was machine-checked (p. 50); close it with the practical comparison of families. Section 4's excursion should be cut as in item 10. In §8, “its results are of five kinds” (p. 67) begins another traversal of the introduction, followed by a page of “What the formalization changed” and open questions (p. 68). Keep a short consequence-focused conclusion and distinguish mathematical questions from unfinished formalization tasks. The detailed campaign chronology and word-count ratio in “Author contribution and use of AI” (pp. 69–70) can remain in the linked process note; preserve the substantive disclosure of AI involvement, verification limits and author responsibility in the article.
