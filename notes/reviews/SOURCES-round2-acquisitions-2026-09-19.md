## 2. Verbatim transcriptions

### (1)/(2) Half-integer Matern as a state-space model

Hartikainen and Sarkka (2010), hartikainen2010kalman, printed p. 3 (Section 4.1 Whittle-Matern
Family), eq. (6)-(9):

  A common class of covariance functions is the Whittle-Matern family [16, 17, 7]

  k_nu(tau) = sigma^2 * 2^(1-nu)/Gamma(nu) * (sqrt(2 nu) tau / l)^nu * K_nu(sqrt(2 nu) tau / l),  (8)

  where l and sigma^2 are the length scale and magnitude hyperparameters ... and nu a parameter
  controlling the smoothness of the process. ... With one-dimensional processes the spectral
  density of the Matern covariance function (8) is

  S(omega) = sigma^2 * 2 pi^(1/2) Gamma(nu+1/2)/Gamma(nu) * lambda^(2 nu) (lambda^2+omega^2)^(-(nu+1/2)),

  where we have denoted lambda = sqrt(2 nu)/l. In this paper we limit our view to cases in which
  nu = p + 1/2, where p is a non-negative integer. Thus, S(omega) is proportional to
  (lambda^2+omega^2)^(-(p+1)). Clearly this is of the desired rational function form (6), and no
  approximation is needed. We can rewrite this as S(omega) is proportional to
  (lambda+i omega)^(-(p+1)) (lambda-i omega)^(-(p+1)), from which we can extract the transfer
  function of a stable Markov process as H(i omega) = (lambda+i omega)^(-(p+1)). The corresponding
  spectral density of the white noise process w(t) is

  q = 2 sigma^2 pi^(1/2) lambda^(2p+1) Gamma(p+1) / Gamma(p+1/2).  (9)

  For example, with p = 1 the corresponding LTI SDE model reads dx(t)/dt = [[0,1],[-lambda^2,
  -2 lambda]] x(t) + [0,1]^T w(t) ...

This is the spectral factorization of (lambda^2+omega^2)^(-(p+1)) into a stable causal transfer
function, exactly as asked, with equation numbers (6)-(9) and the explicit p=1, p=2 state-space
matrices following immediately after on the same printed page.

Sarkka and Solin (2019), sarkka2019applied, printed p. 260 (Section 12.3, "Matern:"), eq.
(12.33)-(12.35), citing Hartikainen and Sarkka by name:

  Matern: A wide class of covariance functions can be defined by the Matern class, which is given
  in its general form as

  C_Mat(t,t) = sigma^2 * 2^(1-nu)/Gamma(nu) * (sqrt(2 nu)|t-t|/l)^nu * K_nu(sqrt(2 nu)|t-t|/l),  (12.33)

  ... For higher-order half-integer Matern covariance functions, the LTI SDE representation is
  given as follows (Hartikainen and Sarkka, 2010): let lambda = sqrt(2 nu)/l and the state
  dimensionality D = nu + 1/2, then the model is given in the so-called companion form as

  F = [companion matrix in a_1 .. a_D], L = [0,...,0,1]^T, (12.34)

  where a_i = (D choose i-1) lambda^(D-i+1) are the binomial coefficients. The measurement model
  matrix is H = (1, 0, ..., 0), the diffusion coefficient is

  q = sigma^2 * [(D-1)!]^2 / (2D-2)! * (2 lambda)^(2D-1),  (12.35)

  and the initial state covariance is the steady-state covariance P_infinity of the model.

and, printed p. 264 (Example 12.7, "Matern SDE model"), the concrete nu=3/2 case, eq.
(12.48)-(12.49):

  For covariance functions of the Matern family for which nu = 3/2, the processes are continuous
  and once differentiable. The covariance function becomes C_Mat(t,t) = sigma^2 (1 + sqrt(3)|t-t|/l)
  exp(-sqrt(3)|t-t|/l).  (12.48) This model has the SDE representation F = [[0,1],[-lambda^2,
  -2 lambda]], L = [0,1]^T, and P_infinity = diag(sigma^2, lambda^2 sigma^2),  (12.49) where
  lambda = sqrt(3)/l. The diffusion constant of the Brownian motion is q = 4 lambda^3 sigma^2. The
  measurement model matrix is H = (1 0).

### (3) Schoenberg characterization -- not independently obtained; carried forward from the held Karlin anchor

Since Schoenberg (1951) itself could not be obtained, the theorem is reported here exactly as
already transcribed and verified in notes/reviews/cone/SOURCES-round2-2026-09-19.md (Part 1,
item 3), from the held karlin1968total, p. 345 (Thm. 3.2(a)), which Karlin own "Notes and
References" (p. 391) attributes to "Schoenberg [1951]" by name (Sections 3 and 4 of Chapter 7 are
elaborations of Schoenberg [1951]):

  THEOREM 3.2. (a) A necessary and sufficient condition that a density function f(u)
  (-infinity < u < infinity) be PF is that the reciprocal of its Laplace transform be an entire
  function of class E2* with gamma + sum a_i^2 > 0.

with class E2* (p. 336) being psi(s) = exp(-gamma s^2 + delta s) s^k prod(1+a_i s)exp(-a_i s),
gamma >= 0, sum a_i^2 < infinity (k = 0, psi(0)=1 for the density case). Karlin Chapter 7 Section 1
(Theorems 2.1, 2.2, 3.1, the sign-change bound Z_-(b) <= Z_-(a) of (3.6)/(3.8)) is the
variation-diminishing route Theorem 3.2 is proved from -- i.e. "PF functions are exactly the
variation-diminishing kernels" is the machinery behind Theorem 3.2 rather than a separately
labelled biconditional on these pages. No new reading of Schoenberg own paper was possible this
round; if the primary is obtained later it should be checked against this secondhand statement for
any discrepancy in the exact hypotheses.

### (10) Triggs and Sdika 2006 / van Vliet, Young and Verbeek 1998 -- bibliographic record plus the one-sentence design change

Triggs and Sdika (2006), triggs2006boundary, printed p. 2365 (abstract): "Young and van Vliet have
designed computationally efficient methods for approximating Gaussian-based convolutions by running
a recursive infinite-impulse-response (IIR) filter forward over the input signal, then running a
second IIR filter backward over the first filter output. To transition between the two filters,
they use a suboptimal heuristic that produces significant amplitude and phase distortion for all
points within about three standard deviations of the right-hand boundary. In this correspondence, a
simple linear transition rule that eliminates this distortion is derived." -- the paper changes the
BOUNDARY CONDITION (the forward/backward hand-off rule), not the poles.

van Vliet, Young and Verbeek (1998), vanvliet1998recursive, printed p. 1 (abstract/Section 1): "The
major drawbacks of our earlier filter [Young and van Vliet 1995, held as young1995recursive] have
been addressed by a new design strategy. The improvements are: improved accuracy ...; arbitrary
order of recursive filter minimizing the L2 or L-infinity norm; closed-form solution of the filter
coefficients as a function of scale (sigma) and recursion order N; improved isotropy and accuracy
for Gaussian derivative filters in n-D space." -- this paper changes the POLES (a new closed-form
coefficient design as a function of sigma and N), not the boundary handling.

## 3. The novelty question

What was actually read, on the article claim: a symmetric law on the line whose radial extension
is the exponent of a self-decomposable law on R^d for every d is a variance mixture of Gaussians
with a self-decomposable mixing law on the half-line, and conversely.

- Barndorff-Nielsen, Maejima and Sato (2006), held in full and read cover to cover for this
  question. It does not use "type G" terminology at all. Its closest statement (p. 9, in the
  discussion after Theorem F) is: "As shown by Bondesson (1992), Theorem 7.3.1, all normal
  variance mixtures where the law of the variance is a generalized Gamma-convolution belong to
  T(R). (Any such mixture equals the law at time 1 of a subordination of Brownian motion by a
  generalized Gamma-convolution subordinator.)" -- this is a SUFFICIENCY statement, for the
  THORIN/GGC mixing class (a strictly smaller class than self-decomposable), in a FIXED DIMENSION
  (R, not "for every d"), with NO CONVERSE and NO QUANTIFICATION over dimension. Nothing closer to
  the article claim was found anywhere else in the paper.
- Aoyama and Maejima (2007) -- read via the proxy (see table, item 6b), not filed. It gives the
  DEFINITION of type G on R^d (its Definition 1.1, verbatim below), and studies the nested classes
  G_m(R^d) obtained by repeatedly asking that the mixing law itself be type G (iterating an
  operator K within a FIXED dimension d) -- a "depth" filtration in m, not a "for every dimension
  d" filtration. No statement quantified over d, and no converse of the
  subordination-gives-self-decomposability direction, was found.
- Barndorff-Nielsen, Pedersen and Sato (2001) -- not obtained; only the abstract (already found in
  the round-2 report) was available: "It is shown that operator self-decomposability and the
  operator version of the class L_m property are inherited from the multivariate subordinator to
  the subordinated process under the condition of operator stability of the subordinand." Again a
  SUFFICIENCY direction, in a FIXED dimension d, for an OPERATOR-STABLE subordinand -- not the
  article dimension-free radial characterization, and (from the abstract alone) no stated converse.

Answer. None of the three papers states the article biconditional, in either direction fully. All
three sit in the same neighbourhood of ideas -- subordinating Brownian motion by a
self-decomposable/GGC/operator-stable law yields a self-decomposable law downstream -- but every
instance found is a SINGLE-DIMENSION SUFFICIENCY statement (sometimes for GGC rather than
self-decomposable mixing, sometimes for an operator-stable subordinand rather than a general one),
never "for every d," and never accompanied by a converse characterizing which laws arise this way.
This is consistent with (though of course does not by itself prove) the round-2 report earlier
assessment that prop:dimension-filtration dimension-free equivalence is a genuine result of this
article rather than a restatement of prior literature -- and this pass adds one paper (Aoyama and
Maejima 2007, read in full) and narrows the other two to specific quoted sentences that the round-2
report could previously only characterize from an abstract or general knowledge of the field.

### The type-G definition and its attribution (items 6/7)

Aoyama and Maejima (2007), Definition 1.1, verbatim: "A probability measure mu0 in I_sym(R^d) is
said to be of type G if its Levy measure nu0 is given by nu0(B) = E[nu(Z^-1 B)], B in B0(R^d),
(1.1) where nu is another Levy measure on R^d and Z is the real-valued standard normal random
variable," introduced with the sentence "Summarizing the discussions in Rosinski (1991) and
Maejima and Rosinski (2001, 2002), we use the following definition of type G distributions on
R^d." THE ATTRIBUTION GIVEN IS ROSINSKI (1991) AND MAEJIMA AND ROSINSKI (2001, 2002) -- NOT MARCUS
(1987); "Marcus" does not appear anywhere in this paper text or reference list (checked directly).
This narrows the task suggested attribution: if Marcus 1987 belongs in module B citation at all,
it is on different grounds than this definition.
