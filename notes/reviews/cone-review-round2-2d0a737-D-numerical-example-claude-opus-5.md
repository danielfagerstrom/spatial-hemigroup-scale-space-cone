# Referee report D — reproduction of the numerical example

**Manuscript**: *Spatial scale space from hemigroup axioms: the admissible cone, its corners and their implementation*, 75 pp. Scope: §7.5–7.6, Example 7.10, Figures 9–11 (pp. 63–69).
**Method**: blind. Every number recomputed from the printed description with my own code (`…/scratchpad/review2/repro/s1…s12`); the author's scripts were never opened. The two designs were read at their sources (Deriche, INRIA report 1893, eq. (38), p. 12; Young & van Vliet, *Signal Processing* 44 (1995), Table 1, p. 141), both held in the library.

## (a) Verdict

The example is well designed and almost entirely correct. I independently reproduced about forty of its numbers, most to the last printed digit: all eighteen entries of the boundedness-threshold table and its growth ratio 2.512; the four matched-bandwidth factors; the four L¹ distances of the weight-2 quadratures (4.2·10⁻², 6.2·10⁻⁴, 4.7·10⁻⁴, 1.9·10⁻⁹); both simplex searches, including the observation that the optimum collapses to a single Matérn stage with a Gaussian; and every number of the recursive-Gaussian passage, down to the interval [+0.0068, +0.0286] for Deriche's slope over the rounding box. The section algebra is exact to machine precision: the forward–backward pair really does have transfer (1+s²Ω²)/(1+t²Ω²) with Ω²=4sin²(ω/2), the ladder telescopes, the lattice variance is 2γt², and p(τ) satisfies p/(1−p)²=τ². The exclusion of the stages from Lindeberg's classification is right, and the paper correctly claims only positivity and mass for them, not non-creation. The two exclusions of the published designs are proofs, not readings of a plot, as the manuscript asserts. Three things need fixing: the distance 0.507 is wrong in its third digit (true value 0.5086 — 0.507 is the integral cut at the window edge, and the same 1.6·10⁻³ bias sits under every distance to the Poisson kernel); the fitted tail rate 1.02 for γ=4 cannot come from the fit the text describes (a log-linear fit of that tail is provably below 1/t); and the real-weight quadrature is under-described at the decisive point, the Thorin mass below the first cell. Two further items concern fairness and self-containedness: Figure 11(d) mixes two bandwidth gauges, and the Deriche numbers cannot be recomputed from what §7.6 prints. Nothing here touches a theorem.

## (c) Findings

**F1. ERROR — p. 68 check 5, and Figure 10 caption p. 66: "distance 0.507".** The L¹ distance between the Poisson kernel (1/π)(1+x²)⁻¹ and ½sech(πx/2) is **0.508593** — three independent computations agree to six digits (scipy quadrature over ℝ split at the crossings 1.129 and 3.166; mpmath at 40 dps; the 2^20 FFT grid). The printed 0.507 is that integral cut at the window edge: ∫_{|x|<400} = 0.507001, and the Poisson kernel carries exactly 1.59·10⁻³ of its mass outside [−400,400]. The number is presented as a mathematical constant ("the distance between the two limits"), so it should read 0.5086. The same bias underlies every printed distance to the Poisson kernel. The *convergence* of the two variants to a common limit, which is the point being made, is unaffected.

**F2. ERROR (as described) — p. 66 check 3, the γ = 4 tail rate.** For a Matérn member of integer index γ the kernel behaves as C x^{γ−1}e^{−x/t}, so d/dx log k = (γ−1)/x − 1/t > −1/t for every x > 0: a log-linear fit of that tail is **below** 1/t on every window, necessarily. Fitting as described I get rate×t = 0.643 (x/t ∈ (3,10)), 0.858 ((10,30)), 0.925 ((20,60)) — never above 1. The printed 1.02 is recovered only by dividing out the prefactor first: fitting log k − (γ−1)log x gives 1.0147 on (10,30), 1.0037 on (20,60). Two consequences: a reader who follows the text will conclude the check fails; and even with the correction, the whole distance of 1.02 from 1 is that window's bias, so the digits carry no information about the member. γ=1 and the 24-range member are clean because their tails carry no prefactor (the latter's smallest atom has weight 2, giving x⁰).

**F3. GAP — p. 68, the real-weight quadrature.** "Cells spaced geometrically between 2/N and 2N with the part of the measure above the last cell carried as a Gaussian coefficient" does not determine the quadrature: the node rule inside a cell and, decisively, the fate of the Thorin mass on (0, 2/N) are missing. That mass is not small — dropping it leaves ≈(2/πN)(log(Nω/2)+1) in the exponent and I get 0.479 at N=4 and 0.0315 at N=256, nineteen times the printed 1.7·10⁻³. With it kept: first cell extended to 0 → 0.152 / 1.63·10⁻³; extra node at half the first boundary → 0.142 / 1.66·10⁻³; extra node at the log-barycentre (2/N)/e → 0.083 / 1.63·10⁻³. The node rule moves N=4 by ~0.01 (geometric 0.152, arithmetic 0.154, log-barycentre 0.153). So 0.154 lies inside the range of honest readings but is not determined; 1.7·10⁻³ is insensitive and reproduces under all of them.

**F4. GAP — p. 69, the Deriche design.** Nothing after "poles 1.783 ± 0.6318 i and 1.723 ± 1.997 i and a cubic numerator in ω²" can be recomputed from the manuscript: "cubic numerator" does not fix the numerator, and the slope, the asymptote, the three zeros, the interval and the 0.05 % all need the four amplitudes a₀ = 1.68, a₁ = 3.735, c₀ = −0.6803, c₁ = −0.2598. With them every number reproduces to the last digit. The interval is a sharp check: slope = a₁ω₀ − a₀b₀ + c₁ω₁ − c₀b₁ = +0.017669, and over the rounding box it runs over [+0.00675, +0.02858] — which matches the manuscript **only because a₀ is printed with two decimals**, its half-width 0.005 supplying 0.0089 of the 0.0109 radius. A reader assuming four decimals throughout would get ±0.0006 and could not explain the manuscript's twenty-fold wider interval.

**F5. IMPRECISE (fairness) — p. 69 last paragraph, Figure 11(d).** The cascade is read "at matched variance" while the published designs are read in their own gauge, and the gauges differ. Young and van Vliet's prototype at q = 1 has **variance 1.177**, not 1; rescaled to unit variance its sup error is **5.98 %**, and the cascade passes it at γ ≈ 7, not γ = 37. Conversely the cascade at its own best dilation is a factor 2.6 better than at matched variance (4.13 % at γ=4 against 10.8 %; 0.49 % at γ=32 against 1.19 %). Deriche's prototype has variance 0.9955, so its 0.05 % is nearly matched — but matching exactly raises it to 0.27 %, which shows how sensitive these sup errors are to a fraction of a percent of dilation. The paragraph's conclusion survives; "falls below 1.04 % at γ = 37" does not, and should carry its gauge.

**F6. GAP (disclosed) — p. 63.** The manuscript says plainly that the signal and seed, the fit windows, the quadrature cells, the L¹ domain and mesh, and the optimizer's start and stopping rule are in the scripts. Welcome honesty, but seven printed numbers therefore cannot be checked by a reader: 4.8·10⁻¹⁴, 2.9·10⁻¹⁴, 5.6/3.1/3.7·10⁻³, 9.7·10⁻³, and 0.040/0.014/0.111. With a signal built to the printed description I get the same orders and the structural facts that matter (refinement moves records only by rounding error; the Euler march halves, ratios 2.01/2.08/2.10; largest Poisson difference 0.108 against 0.111), but up to a factor three where the finest feature dominates. Two sentences naming the signal's components and the seed would close it.

**F7. GAP — p. 69, "the identity of Proposition 5.2(1) extends to complex θ of positive real part".** The whole Young–van Vliet exclusion rests on this, and §7.6 asserts it and then only confirms it numerically (I confirm: 1.25·10⁻¹¹). Remark 7.5 (p. 60) already proves the same step for the pair 1 ± i "by the same differentiation under the integral sign" — cross-reference it, or state a one-line lemma.

**F8. IMPRECISE — p. 64, "the kernels from the origin are convolution powers of the two-sided geometric kernel".** True of the Matérn members; the 24-range member is a convolution *product* of 24 different geometric kernels. Still inside Lindeberg's class, so the argument is unaffected.

**F9. PRESENTATION — p. 65, Figure 9 caption.** The caption says the key "saturates at ±0.055"; the key in the figure reads ±0.056. More substantively, the largest Poisson difference is 0.111, twice the saturation, so that panel is clipped over a visible area and the caption does not say so.

**F10. Design (positive) — the two exclusions are proofs.** For Young and van Vliet: at x = (2n+1)π/1.40586 the cosine is −1 and k(x) = 2e^{−1.1668x} − 4e^{−1.10783x} < 0 because the real pole decays faster; then the uniqueness of the Lévy pair (Remark 7.3). Correct, and stronger than stated — the manuscript says the second term is "eventually the larger", but 2e^{−(b₀−b₁)x} < 4 for all x > 0, so it is already larger at the first such point, x = 2.2346, where k = −0.1890. For Deriche: H(0) = 2.507 > 0 and H(ω) ∼ −0.0353/ω² < 0, a sign change of an explicit rational function, made independent of the printed digits by the interval bound. That an infinitely divisible transform has no zero finishes it; and "no stable filter takes its output at σ to its output at 2σ" is right — the ratio's poles are at {4.208, 5.076, 9.815}/σ and its zeros at half those frequencies, so nothing cancels. The 2000 random draws are correctly labelled as proving nothing. One remark on a different check: **check 4 probes the threshold from the convergence side only** — a divergent π⁻¹∫₀^Λ e^{−F} shows the inversion integral diverges, not that no representative is bounded, which is Corollary 3.9(1)'s content for c ≤ 1. The manuscript does not claim otherwise, and the table's design (two families at each of c = 0.8, 1, 1.2, so that members with the same c but different Thorin measures show the same growth exponent) is a genuinely good control, which I confirm.
