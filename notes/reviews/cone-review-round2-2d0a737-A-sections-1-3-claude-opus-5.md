# Referee report A — §1.1 and Table 1 (pp. 4–6), §2 and §3 (pp. 7–21)

## (a) Verdict on the block

The mathematics of the block is sound. I found no false statement and no failing step in §2 or §3. I recomputed the factor two of Lemma 3.5 twice, symbolically and numerically (residual 2·10⁻⁷ at five points; the same identity with 1 in place of ½ is wrong by O(1)); I verified that the constant of Lemma 3.1(1) is exactly Euler's, since the proof's own definition collapses to Cin(1) + Ci(1) = γ; I checked the layer cake, the domain sandwich, the injectivity argument and the classification of the extreme rays in Proposition 3.3; and I read Sato's Theorems 28.4 and 53.8 at the page images and checked Proposition 3.7 and Corollary 3.9 against them, including the folding factor 2, which an independent route confirms (Sato's κ for a Cin ray of step τ ≥ 1 is e^{−γ}/τ, and the Fourier decay of e^{−Cin(τω)} forces exactly the coefficient e^{−γ}/(πτ) of log(1/|x|); the manuscript's own §7.10 table grows by 0.82 per factor 100 against the predicted 0.823). What is wrong is of three kinds. One citation is dead: DLMF (6.13.3) and (6.13.4) do not exist, §6.13 being *Zeros*. Three hypotheses of the two Sato theorems are used without being matched — Sato's driftless form (53.14), Sato's one-sided continuity normalisation of the k-function, and the nonvanishing of the constant in (53.28)/(53.30) that the comparison "≍" needs — all three of which hold here, but the whole point of Lemma 3.6 and of §1.1's "at the source's own letter" is that such matchings are made on the page. And there is an accounting failure that reaches the headline: the boundedness threshold is asserted without qualification in §1 (p. 2), §3's introduction (p. 12), Table 3 (p. 38) and §8 (p. 70), but Corollary 3.9 is proved only for finite k(0+), so two rows of Table 3 rest on nothing the paper proves. §2 is close to self-contained, but three results of the line paper are used by number beyond the four it declares, one of them inside the proof of Lemma 3.5, and the Fourier convention that proof needs is never fixed. None of this threatens a result; all of it is repairable in a page.

## (b) Findings

**F1 — ERROR (citation). p. 13, Remark 3.2, last sentence.** "([11], (6.2.13) for the identity, and (6.2.20) with the expansions (6.13.3) and (6.13.4) for the rate)." DLMF §6.13 is *Zeros*; it contains 6.13.1 (the real zero of Ei) and 6.13.2 (the zeros of Ci and si) and nothing else. The expansions of the auxiliary functions f and g, which are what turn 6.2.20 into Ci(z) = O(1/z), are **6.12.3 and 6.12.4**. The other two anchors are correct: 6.2.13 is Ci(z) = −Cin(z) + ln z + γ and 6.2.20 is Ci = f sin − g cos. *Repair:* replace (6.13.3), (6.13.4) by (6.12.3), (6.12.4). (Checked at dlmf.nist.gov/6.2, /6.12, /6.13.)

**F2 — IMPRECISE. §1.1 p. 4 ("of §3, everything") and p. 6 ("Every other numbered statement is machine-checked"), against Remark 3.8 (p. 21) and Remark 3.2 (p. 13).** Remark 3.8 is a numbered statement of §3 which the manuscript itself says is not proved: "the induction it summarises is not carried out here." §1.1 nevertheless claims all of §3, and its list of seven unformalized things does not name it; the only remarks excepted are 4.3 and 5.11. Separately, Remark 3.2 rests directly on DLMF, a cited fact with no row in Table 1, whose caption says "A result not listed uses no cited fact directly, apart from the regularity of a self-decomposable density". Both sentences are false as a reader would read them. *Repair:* name Remark 3.8 in "What is not formalized" (or exclude remarks marked as descriptions from the count), and either give DLMF a row or restrict the caption to numbered propositions, lemmas and theorems.

**F3 — IMPRECISE, and the gap reaches the headline. Corollary 3.9 (p. 21) against p. 2, p. 12, p. 38 Table 3, p. 39 §5 introduction, p. 70 §8.** Corollary 3.9 assumes "c := k(0+) is finite and positive", and Proposition 3.7 says "nothing is claimed when c = ∞". But the threshold is stated unconditionally wherever it is advertised — "bounded at the origin exactly when its profile starts above 1 (Corollary 3.9)" (p. 2); "bounded exactly when that value exceeds 1" (p. 12); the same in §8 (p. 70). Worse, Table 3 (p. 38) has a *bounded at 0* column which §5's introduction attributes to Corollary 3.9, and two of its five rows — Student-t and stable — are families with k(0+) = ∞, as the manuscript itself states on p. 20. Those two entries are asserted and proved nowhere I could find. *Repair:* add a clause to Corollary 3.9, "if c = ∞ then φ₁ has a C^∞ representative", on Sato's Theorem 28.4(ii) (p. 191) — a fourth clause of a theorem the ledger already admits — after which the unqualified sentences become true as printed.

**F4 — GAP. p. 20, proof of Proposition 3.7, "By Lemma 3.6 the law in question is theirs."** Sato's Theorem 53.8 is stated for a selfdecomposable law "satisfying (53.14)", where (53.14) (p. 408) is μ̂(z) = exp[∫_ℝ (e^{izx} − 1)k(x)|x|⁻¹dx]: no Gaussian part, **finite variation**, and **drift zero**, with (53.15) requiring ∫_{|x|<1}k(x)dx < ∞. The proof verifies none of this. Lemma 3.6 proves only ∫_ℝ(1 ∧ x²)k₂(x)|x|⁻¹dx < ∞, strictly weaker than (53.15). Both missing conditions hold — k ≤ c on (0,∞) gives ∫_0^1 k₂ ≤ c/2 < ∞ whenever c is finite, and the drift vanishes by symmetry — but each is a step, and the finite variation is exactly what the hypothesis c < ∞ is buying. *Repair:* one sentence in Lemma 3.6, under the hypothesis c < ∞ it already carries.

**F5 — GAP. p. 19, Lemma 3.6, "a two-sided profile in the sense of the probabilistic sources".** Sato's *k-function* is not merely a monotone function: it is by definition the version "right-continuous on (−∞,0) and left-continuous on (0,∞)" ([37], p. 403, repeated in the hypothesis of Theorem 28.4, p. 191). Lemma 3.6 verifies nonnegativity, the two monotonicities and the integral condition, and stops. A nonincreasing k need not be left-continuous, and the manuscript elsewhere insists a profile is fixed only up to a null set and declines to choose a version (proof of Lemma 3.1(2): "a right-continuous version of k is neither available nor needed"). Nothing mathematical is lost — x ↦ k(x−) changes neither the exponent, nor K, nor L, nor k(0+) — but matching hypotheses is precisely what Lemma 3.6 exists to do, and §1.1 advertises these names as used "at the source's own letter". *Repair:* pass to the left-continuous modification in the lemma and record the three invariances.

**F6 — GAP. p. 20, Proposition 3.7 and its proof; p. 5, §1.1 on the three names.** The proposition concludes a two-sided comparison, φ₁(x) ≍ |x|^{c−1}K(|x|) for c < 1 and ≍ L(|x|) at c = 1, and says it takes "the order of the singularity … and not the constant in Sato's formula". But f ~ A·g yields f ≍ g only if A ≠ 0, and Sato's constants are κ sin(k(0+)π)/(Γ(c) sin cπ) in (53.28) and (κ/π)cos(c′π/2) in (53.30), either of which vanishes for admissible two-sided data (c′ = ±1 kills the second). Here both are positive, and for exactly the reason the folding produces: k₂(0±) = c/2 gives sin(k(0+)π)/sin(cπ) = 1/(2cos(cπ/2)) > 0 for 0 < c < 1, and c′ = 0 gives cos(c′π/2) = 1. The proof does establish c′ = 0 but never says the conclusion needs the constant nonzero. *Repair:* one clause; it also strengthens the paper's own point that the factor 2 and the symmetry are load-bearing.

**F7 — IMPRECISE. p. 20, statement of Proposition 3.7.** "…and put c := k(0+) ∈ (0,∞]" asserts c > 0 rather than hypothesising it. By p. 10 the zero exponent *is* admissible as an exponent — only (ND) excludes it as a family — and it has k ≡ 0, c = 0, and no kernel in the sense used (its law is δ₀). *Repair:* add F ≢ 0, or c > 0, as Corollary 3.9 correctly does.

**F8 — GAP (self-containment). p. 7, §2 opening, against p. 18.** §2 declares "Four further results are used and only cited" and names them. At least three more line-paper results are used by number and are neither restated nor listed: **Theorem 5.2 of [14]**, the truncation inequality, invoked on p. 18 to get μ{|x| > R} = O(R⁻²) and hence the finite first absolute moment on which all of Lemma 3.5 rests; Lemma 2.11 of [14] (§4); Lemma 5.1 of [14] (§7). The first is inside my block and is not a triviality: without it the left-hand side of (3.2) is not known to be a finite measure and the recombination step has nothing to apply Proposition 2.7 of [14] to. *Repair:* state the truncation inequality, or the consequence actually used, as a fifth item of §2, and correct the count.

**F9 — GAP. §2.1, p. 7, against the proof of Lemma 3.5, p. 18.** §2.1 fixes only the transform of a *symmetric* measure, μ̂(ω) = ∫cos(ωx)μ(dx). The proof of Lemma 3.5 works throughout with non-symmetric finite measures (ν = μ ∗ κ_τ and its translate) and with a signed measure, under ρ̂(ω) = ∫e^{−iωx}ρ(dx) — the sign is visible in ν̂_τ(ω) = e^{iωτ}ν̂(ω) and κ̂_τ(ω) = (1 − e^{−iωτ})/(iω) — and applies the cited uniqueness (Proposition 2.7 of [14]) to those non-symmetric measures. This is the one place in the paper where the sign matters, and the convention is introduced silently. *Repair:* fix the general convention in §2.1 and say the cited uniqueness is for it.

**F10 — PRESENTATION. p. 5, §1.1, the disclosure of the false admission.** The anchors are right — (53.25) and (53.28) are both on p. 411 — and the disclosure says what was omitted, that it was false, who found it, and that it is now at the letter. What it does not say is whether anything the paper *prints* changed: whether Proposition 3.7's statement, Corollary 3.9, or any downstream result was affected, or whether the error was confined to the formalisation. A reader judging the development's honesty has to guess, and the guess is the whole interest of the disclosure. *Repair:* one clause on what did and did not change in the printed mathematics.

**F11 — PRESENTATION. Table 1 and its caption, p. 6.** The caption says the first of the last two rows is used "with the polar criterion of self-decomposability in dimension d (A7) and the Lévy–Khintchine representation in dimension d (A3) beside it". A7 has no row and is introduced nowhere in §1.1, whose accounting is "the cited facts of the line paper and three more"; and A3's row is labelled "symmetric Lévy–Khintchine representation", the statement on the line, not the representation in dimension d. **F12 — PRESENTATION. p. 13, proof of Lemma 3.1(1).** The chain |cos v − (1 − v²/2)| ≤ (5/96)v⁴ ⟹ |(1 − cos v)/v − v/2| ≤ (5/96)v³ ⟹ |Cin(z) − ¼z²| ≤ (5/96)z⁴ is correct but doubly slack and unexplained: the natural bound on the first line is v⁴/24, and integrating the second gives (5/384)z⁴. ## (c) Checked and found sound

- **Lemma 3.1(1).** Evenness, monotonicity, Cin(z) ≤ z²/4, the O(z⁴) expansion, the splitting at v = 1, the integration by parts, |Cin(z) − log z − C| ≤ 2/z for z ≥ 1, and the substitution v = ωx identifying the unit-step exponent with Cin(τω). All correct.
- **Remark 3.2.** Correct, and in fact exact: the proof's C = Cin(1) + sin 1 − ∫_1^∞ v⁻²sin v dv reduces by one integration by parts to Cin(1) + Ci(1) = γ (DLMF 6.2.13). Verified to 30 digits.
- **Lemma 3.1(2).** The generalised inverse gives ϖ((x,∞)) = k(x+) exactly, so the asserted sandwich holds; the discarded infinite atom at the origin, σ-finiteness on [ε,∞), and the Tonelli exchange are right, and the two isolated properties of k are indeed the only ones used.
- **Proposition 3.3.** The representation is *elementary*, not Choquet-theoretic, and the paper says so ("the proposition below does not use it"); it is an explicit linear bijection and uniqueness is proved, not invoked. I checked both layer-cake identities; the sandwich ½(τ∧1)² + log₊τ ≤ τ² ∧ (1+log₊τ) ≤ 2[½(τ∧1)² + log₊τ] (also verified numerically on (0,20]); the three-regime domination of τ² ∧ (1+log₊τ) by Cin(τ|ω|), with the constants 19/96 and Cin(1) and the "half of log τ" threshold; the two-step passage from tails agreeing a.e. to equality of measures, including why a direct π-system argument fails; the extremality of the Gaussian ray and of each Cin ray (the "nonincreasing and nondecreasing hence constant" step is correct on the conull set); and the classification of all other extreme rays, where τ := inf{s : ϖ((0,s]) > 0} is shown to carry ϖ and to be positive and of finite mass. One step is used and not stated: in the "onto" direction, ϖ((x,∞)) < ∞ for every x > 0, which follows because τ² ∧ (1+log₊τ) is bounded below by a positive constant on (x,∞).
- **Lemma 3.5 and its factor two.** Recomputed from scratch. With ρ̂(ω) = ∫e^{−iωx}ρ(dx): ν̂ − ν̂_τ = c(ω)(1−e^{−iωτ})(1−e^{iωτ})/(iω) = −2i(1−cos ωτ)c(ω)/ω, while the transform of 2xμ(dx) is 2ic′(ω) = −2i(1−cos τω)c(ω)/ω by the delay ODE (3.3). So (3.2) is right. At densities, ν has density μ((x−τ,x]) and ν_τ has density μ((x,x+τ]), difference 2P(x) − P(x−τ) − P(x+τ); the ½ is correct, and differentiating gives the second display. The recombination into two positive finite measures and the check at ω = 0 are both right. Verified numerically by inverting e^{−Cin(ω)} on a tapered grid: the residual of x p(x) − P(x) + ½[P(x−1) + P(x+1)] is below 2·10⁻⁷ at x = 0.3, 0.7, 1.4, 2.3, 3.1, whereas the same expression with 1 in place of ½ is off by 0.41 to 1.0.
- **Lemma 3.6.** The structural conditions, the exponent identity (the two factors 2 do cancel), k₂(x) + k₂(−x) = k(x), and k₂(0±) = c/2 with sum c and difference 0, are correct; and k = 2h against Sato's Corollary 15.11 (p. 95), which I read, is the right reading. See F4, F5.
- **Proposition 3.7 against the sources, read at the page images.** Sato Thm 28.4 (p. 191) gives, for N < c ≤ N+1 with c > 1, a C^{N−1} extension — exactly "extends continuously and has N−1 derivatives"; the drift γ₀ is irrelevant there because the extension is global, and is zero here by symmetry anyway. Sato Thm 53.8 (pp. 410–411) with (53.24) requires c < ∞, k(0+) > 0 and k(0−) > 0, all supplied by the folding from c > 0; (53.25)–(53.26) are, under the folding, verbatim the paper's K and L, since c − k(y) − k(−y) becomes c − k_folded(y); (53.28)–(53.29) give two-sided |x|^{c−1}K(|x|) for c < 1 and (53.30) gives L(|x|) at c = 1. The assignment of regimes to theorems in §3.4's "cited fact" paragraph is correct. K ≥ 1 and the convergence of both integrals are correct. The unboundedness example k(x) = ½ − 1/log(1/x) is correct: K(y) ≍ log(1/y), kernel of order |x|^{−1/2}log(1/|x|). The Cin specialisation is correct in both branches: K ≡ max(1, 1/τ) on (0, min(τ,1)), and L(x) = log(1/|x|) for τ ≥ 1, L(x) = τ⁻¹log(τ/|x|) + τ⁻¹ − 1 for τ < 1.
- **Corollary 3.9.** N = ⌈c⌉ − 1 does satisfy N < c ≤ N+1 for every finite c > 1, with N ≥ 1. The measure comparison that upgrades "the canonical representative is unbounded" to "no representative is bounded" is correct, and it is right to run it through φ₁((0,ε)) rather than pointwise. Below and at the threshold the lower bounds diverge as ε ↓ 0, so c = 1 is genuinely on the unbounded side.
- **The three regimes on explicit members, numerically.** With k(x) = 2γe^{−x} the exponent is γ log(1+ω²) and the kernel is |x|^{γ−1/2}K_{γ−1/2}(|x|)/(√π Γ(γ)2^{γ−1/2}) — transform checked at two frequencies for γ = ¼, ½, 1. Then γ = ¼ (c = ½): φ₁(x)/|x|^{c−1} → ≈0.39, the singular regime; γ = ½ (c = 1): φ₁(x)/log(1/|x|) → 1/π, matching K₀(|x|)/π and the exact prediction (κ/π)K(0+) = 1/π from (53.27) and (53.30); γ = ¾ and 1: bounded, tending to 0.809 and 0.5 = ½e^{−|x|}|₀. For the Cin ray at τ = 1 the inverted kernel satisfies p(x) − (e^{−γ}/π)log(1/|x|) → 0.2757, confirming the threshold regime and Sato's κ = e^{−γ}/τ, hence the folding factor 2 from an independent direction. Table 3 (p. 38) and Propositions 5.2(1), 5.3 are consistent with Corollary 3.9(2) (threshold at γ = ½, Bessel K₀ there), and the §7.10 table is consistent with all three regimes.
- **§2 as a restatement, item by item.** The folded profile and the factor conventions between the two-sided Lévy density and k *are* supplied (p. 9, Remark 2.4); F, the symbol B = ωF′ and ϖ = −dk are supplied (§2.3, (2.3)); the hemigroup and what it weakens are supplied (A6 and p. 8); the canonical gauge and "the kernel at canonical scale t" are supplied (§2.4). Theorem 2.5 is restated with its hypotheses and with the normalisation that makes (χ,F) unique. The gaps I found are F8 and F9, plus the minor notational one that Remark 3.4 (p. 17) uses a chain ID_s ⊃ SD_s ⊃ GGC_s of which only ND_s = LE_s is defined in §2 — and that one is a chain of classes of laws while ND_s is a class of exponents.
- **§1.1's substance, where it is not F2/F3/F11.** The list of what is proved, assumed and not formalized is unusually explicit, the per-name narrowings are stated rather than hidden, and the printed `#print axioms` block is the kind of evidence that makes the claim checkable. The three names for the density at the origin are used within their hypotheses as far as the printed text goes, subject to F4, F5, F6; A3's uniqueness clause is used within its hypotheses in Proposition 3.3.

## (d) Not settled

1. **The formalisation itself.** I reviewed only the printed text and did not open the accompanying repository, so every claim of §1.1 about what is machine-checked is unverified by me. Settled by running `lake build` and the axiom guard against the trust-boundary file and reading the `\lean` tags of §§2–3 — which is what F2 and F3 make me want, since both are failures of the *printed* accounting rather than of the Lean.
2. **Remark 3.8's echoes.** The claimed orders (a logarithmic singularity in p′_τ at ±τ, a kink of order |x ∓ τ| log|x ∓ τ| in p_τ) are consistent with Figure 1 and with the delay equation, and the manuscript disclaims proof. I did not verify them. Settled by carrying out the induction on Lemma 3.5, one order per lattice step, which looks routine.
3. **Whether the k(0+) = ∞ members really are bounded at the origin** in the sense Table 3 asserts — I believe so via Sato Thm 28.4(ii), but the paper does not say it and I did not check the Student-t and stable rows against §§5.4–5.5. Settled by F3's repair.
4. **The line paper [14].** Not available to me, so every "Proposition 2.3 of [14]"-style citation was taken on trust, including the truncation inequality of F8 and the quadratic-growth lemma. A referee with the companion paper should check that its Theorem 5.2 gives μ{|x| > R} = O(R⁻²) under the stated flatness and that Lemma 2.10 is the finiteness used on p. 15.
