\begin{abstract}
Gaussian scale space is the classical answer to the question of which smoothing kernels can
serve a multi-scale representation. A companion paper showed that on the line the answer is
much larger when the semigroup requirement of the axioms is weakened to a cascade, a
two-parameter hemigroup: the admissible kernels are the symmetric self-decomposable laws,
each given by a Gaussian coefficient and a nonincreasing profile of displacement sizes. This
paper describes that class for a reader who computes with it, in three steps. The first is
its shape. The admissible exponents form a convex cone in which every member is a unique
superposition of the Gaussian and the members whose profile is a unit step. Subordination maps
the time-causal scale spaces of the same axioms injectively into the cone, and the image
consists of the members whose radial extension is self-decomposable in every dimension. The
second is the familiar families in it: the \Matern{} kernels of spatial statistics, the
Student-t laws and the symmetric stable laws are located and compared by their moments, their
tails and their behaviour at the origin. The third is computation. For signals on the line the
cascade composes exactly over any finite set of scales, and the \Matern{} families of integer
index are exactly those whose stages are cascades of identical first-order recursive filters,
run forward and backward. The families computed in this way do not reach the stable members
even in the limit, and the sampling of the signal is a separate approximation. A numerical
example checks the statements and finds that the continuous prototypes of two recursive
Gaussian filters in common use are the kernels of no such cascade. Locality and the
non-creation of local extrema are outside the paper. Most of the results of the first two
steps are machine-checked in Lean~4, and those of the third are proved in prose.
\end{abstract}
